An embedding is a dense vector that represents the meaning of a word, sentence or document. Embedding models are trained so that texts with similar meaning are placed close together in the vector space, usually measured with cosine similarity. Sentence embedding models such as those built with contrastive learning are trained on pairs of related sentences, pulling matching pairs together and pushing unrelated pairs apart. Unlike sparse representations such as bag of words, embeddings capture synonyms and paraphrases, so a query about privacy can match a passage about protecting personal information even without shared keywords. The dimensionality is fixed, commonly between three hundred and fifteen hundred values. Embeddings power semantic search, clustering, classification and the retrieval step of retrieval augmented generation. Their weakness is that the dimensions are not interpretable, and similarity scores are relative rankings rather than calibrated probabilities of relevance.
