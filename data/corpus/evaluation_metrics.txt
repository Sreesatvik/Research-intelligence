Retrieval systems are evaluated against a set of queries with known relevant documents. Recall at k measures the fraction of relevant documents that appear in the top k results. Precision at k measures the fraction of the top k results that are relevant. Mean reciprocal rank looks at the position of the first relevant result, rewarding systems that place a correct answer near the top. Building a small but trustworthy evaluation set is more valuable than relying on intuition, because it shows whether a change such as a new embedding model or a different chunk size actually helps. For generated answers, evaluation also checks grounding, meaning whether every claim is supported by the retrieved passages, and citation correctness. Latency and cost should be tracked alongside quality so improvements are not bought at unacceptable expense.
