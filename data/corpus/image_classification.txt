Convolutional neural networks achieve strong results on image classification by applying small learned filters across an image. Early layers detect edges and textures, middle layers combine them into parts, and deeper layers recognize whole objects. Weight sharing across positions makes these networks efficient and gives them translation invariance, meaning a cat is recognized wherever it appears in the picture. Pooling layers reduce resolution while keeping the strongest responses. Training uses large labeled datasets such as ImageNet and data augmentation like random crops and flips to reduce overfitting. More recently, vision transformers split an image into patches and process them with self attention, matching or surpassing convolutional networks when trained on enough data. Pretrained vision models are commonly fine tuned for new tasks with far less labeled data.
