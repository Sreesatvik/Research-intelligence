Retrieval augmented generation combines a retriever with a language model. Instead of relying only on what the model memorized during training, the system first searches an external document collection for passages relevant to the question, then places those passages into the prompt and asks the model to answer using them. This reduces hallucination, allows answers to use private or recent documents, and makes it possible to cite sources. The quality of the final answer depends heavily on retrieval quality: if the retriever returns irrelevant passages, the model may produce a fluent but wrong answer. A well designed system also instructs the model to say when the retrieved evidence is insufficient. Common improvements include reranking candidates with a cross encoder, tuning chunk size and overlap, combining keyword and dense search, and filtering by metadata such as date or author.
