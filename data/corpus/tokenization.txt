Tokenization splits raw text into units called tokens before a language model can process it. Modern models use subword tokenizers such as byte pair encoding, which start from individual characters and repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pairs. Common words become a single token, while rare words are broken into smaller pieces. This keeps the vocabulary at a manageable size, typically between thirty thousand and one hundred thousand entries, while still being able to represent any input text, including misspellings and new words. Each token is then mapped to an integer identifier, which is used to look up a learned embedding vector. The choice of tokenizer affects cost, because pricing and context limits are measured in tokens rather than words. It also affects behavior on numbers, code and languages that are underrepresented in the training data, where a single word may be split into many tokens.
